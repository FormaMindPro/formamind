# acceptance-market - the acceptance layer, deployed outward

## The divide

An interview persuades the human toward their own truth - the two interests
coincide, and that coincidence is the safety of `acceptance-vector`. Marketing
persuades a person toward a seller's yes: the interests diverge, and at every
point of divergence the easy move is a manipulation. The layer therefore
deploys with the same five-fold structure and stricter guards: everything
below is the vector read outward; nothing below is licensed by the vector's
honesty alone.

## The unfolding - the five layers as marketing's working set

- **Barrier - the message material.** Beauty is the image the offer opens by;
  simplicity is what it does, in numbered steps; individuality is the fit to
  the named case; honesty is the price, the conditions and the proof;
  naturalness is the exit - reversibility, low commitment, a start that asks
  for almost nothing.
- **Extreme - the overdose of creative and promise.** The generalized image
  (a stock-photo dream), the over-simplified promise (steps minus the
  essential), the faked "for you" (a segment costume), the arranged trust
  (proof placed for effect, not accuracy), the asserted effortlessness ("no
  effort!" over a real cost). Every artifact is checked against its own
  overdose before release, and the check is written next to the artifact.
- **Mood - the tone of channel and packaging.** The brand voice holds the
  image firmly; the offer shapes re-slice with the market's answers;
  onboarding adjusts softly; proof waits patiently. A channel that cannot
  hold the needed mood does not get the message.
- **Feeling - the audience's stake.** Fear of wasted money and time; sadness
  over past purchases that failed; anger at being treated as a segment;
  disgust at marketing falseness; joy at what finally fits. The stake is what
  the message meets - meet it, never argue it.
- **Segments - cut by the dominant stake.** Segments are cut by which barrier
  the person asks about first, not by demographics alone: demographics
  explain reach, the barrier explains the message.

## Research - hearing the barrier distribution

The audience tells you where it stands; research is structured listening, not
surveillance.

- **Interviews.** The `grilling` technique on real customers and refusers:
  rounds along their decision's frontier, meet-not-march, one particular per
  overdosed answer. A refusal interview is worth three purchases - the
  refuser stands at the exact barrier the message missed.
- **Language mining.** The words map (`acceptance-vector`, "The words")
  applied to what the audience already wrote: reviews, support tickets,
  messages, forum threads. Count the words of each cloud - image words, steps
  words, for-me words, guarantee words, cost-me words; the distribution of
  words is the distribution of barriers. Quotes are kept verbatim, with
  source, in the research file.
- **The two rules.** (1) A feeling is data about its bearer, never evidence:
  what the audience feels explains what to meet, never what to claim - claims
  need their own proof. (2) Every statement about the audience carries a
  verbatim quote as its particular; a segment description without a quote is
  a guess and is marked as one.
- **No covert work.** Nothing is gathered the audience would not knowingly
  show: no hidden recording, no scraped private spaces, no fabricated
  research questions. Research that would embarrass the researcher read aloud
  by the researched is not run.

## The guards - stricter than the interview's

- **Truth before commitment, hardened.** Price, full conditions and the exit
  path stand before the CTA - not after the click, not in the footer, not "on
  request".
- **No manufactured sociality.** No invented urgency, no synthetic reviews,
  no rented crowd. Social proof is verbatim and attributable, or absent.
- **Real reversibility, not declared.** Whatever exit is promised is walked
  through by the project itself before it is promised; an untested refund
  path is a claim without proof.
- **Friction may fall; doubt may not.** A creative may carry the image
  (beauty) and ease the steps (simplicity) - it may not pre-answer the
  honesty barrier with claims. Doubt goes to proof, never to slogans; a
  headline that resolves doubt is lying about where proof lives.
- **The reversal, hardened.** At the commit moment (checkout, sign-up) the
  vector ends: what "yes" binds, what it costs, how to leave - one screen,
  plain words, before the button.
- **Symmetry, hardened.** The campaign must survive the buyer reading this
  skill and the research file about people like them.

## Strategy - positioning by barrier

**Positioning is the choice of which barrier you answer first and best.**
Every competitor whose product works at all answers all five eventually; a
position is the one the market hits first when it hears your name. Choose it
from the research, not from preference: the dominant stake of the dominant
segment names the barrier your message opens by, and the other four stand
behind it in the acceptance order (`acceptance-vector`).

**Message architecture - one of two shapes.** A small offer: one spine in the
acceptance order, image → steps → fit → proof → exit, each block carrying
exactly one barrier and handing to the next. A larger system: one artifact
per barrier - the ad carries the image, the landing page the steps and the
fit, the proof and conditions stand before the CTA (the guards above), the
exit is a feature of the offer. Never two barriers in one block: a block that
answers two answers neither, and a block that answers a barrier past the
person's standing place bounces (`acceptance-vector` - reorder and wrap,
never push).

**The funnel is the acceptance order.** image → steps → fit → proof → exit:
the stages are the barriers, in the order a person passes them. A stage's
only job is the next barrier - the ad's job is to make them ask what it does,
the steps' job is the trying on, the proof's job is the yes. A stage that
asks for the yes early has skipped the person's standing place and pays for
it in bounce; a stage exit is not a failure of the audience (see Metrics
below).

**Channels are chosen by the mood they can hold.** The mood rides with the
channel, not with the copy alone: a channel of interruption cannot hold
patience - proof bends toward the quick claim; a channel of idle browsing
cannot hold mercilessness - the image blurs. Assign each stage of the funnel
to a channel that can hold its mood (the unfolding above); where no channel
holds it, the message changes shape or waits.

**Offer design - truth before commitment as the feature.** What it costs,
what it binds, how you leave: stated on the offer's face, part of its value -
this is the offer's naturalness material (reversibility), not legal hygiene.
An offer with a real, visible exit competes on the fifth barrier honestly; an
offer that hides conditions does not compete - it postpones the honesty
barrier past the click, which the guards above forbid.

## Tactics - artifacts in barrier order

**The page template - one block per barrier, a guard line riding each:**

| Block | Barrier | Guard line riding it |
|---|---|---|
| Opening image | beauty | an image of the real thing - no stock dream |
| Steps | simplicity | the numbered steps include the one that costs |
| Fit | individuality | one named case, verbatim from the research |
| Proof | honesty | price, conditions and sources in the block, not below it |
| Exit | naturalness | the tested way out, visible - never "on request" |

The table is a shape, not a script: the blocks may be sections of one page or
pages of one sequence, and a guard line is written into the artifact itself,
next to the material it guards - the check rides the artifact, as the
unfolding says.

**Headlines.** A headline is beauty material: it carries the image and stops
there. It may open a question, never close one - a headline that resolves
doubt is lying about where proof lives (the guards above). Steps, price and
conditions never ride a headline; they ride their own blocks.

**Proof placement.** Proof stands where the person stands when they need it:
the fit block - the named case; the proof block - sources, numbers,
verbatims; the commit moment - the reversal block (the guards above). Proof
placed after the click is proof placed after it was needed.

**The exit as a feature.** The exit is designed, named and priced into the
offer: what "yes" binds, how to leave, what leaving costs - on its face, in
plain words. A real exit is naturalness material and a differentiator; a
hidden exit is asserted effortlessness (the extremes above).

**A/B discipline.** Variants test the material of a barrier - another image,
another arrangement of steps, another named case. They never test hiding a
fact, delaying a price, or moving the exit further from the button: that is
not a variant, it is a guard breach. An experiment that passes only by
lowering doubt is discarded with its result.

**One example per barrier, coined anew.** Illustrations of shape, not stock
phrases: beauty - "the Saturday version of your site, live on Monday";
simplicity - "three steps: you send the brief, we build, you click publish";
individuality - "for the studio of one that hates retainer calls"; honesty -
"€X a month, cancel with one click, export everything"; naturalness - "start
with the free page - the account is yours to keep or drop". Each campaign
coins its own; a reused example is the phrase library the guards above
forbid (`acceptance-vector` - coined anew, never a library).

## Metrics - acceptance without dishonesty

**Instrument the barriers, not the persuader.** Each stage of the funnel is
measured as a pass-rate into the next: attention → comprehension (the image
grasped) → fit (tried on) → proof (checked) → commit (the yes, with the
reversal block before the button). A pass-rate reads as "how many crossed
this barrier" - never as "how well we moved them across".

**A drop point names the barrier, not the audience.** Where people leave,
they stand: a drop at comprehension says the image did not open into steps; a
drop at fit says the named case was not their case; a drop at proof says the
proof came late, thin, or arranged. Bounce is data about the message - the
fix is the barrier's material, re-coined; never the audience's patience,
intelligence or taste.

**Refund and exit usage is a health metric.** High exits after the yes mean
the offer oversold: a message defect, not a buyer defect - the artifact goes
back against its own overdose (the extremes above), and the exit is left
easy. A falling refund rate after the exit was made more visible is the fifth
barrier working; a falling refund rate after the exit was made harder is a
guard breach wearing a good number.

**No dark-pattern KPIs.** No metric rewards doubt lowered: no commit-rate
improved by hiding the price, no engagement counted from frustration, no
urgency-driven spikes. A dashboard line that rises only through a guard
breach is removed together with the practice that fed it.

**The reading rule.** A feeling is data about its bearer, never evidence; a
metric is data about the message, never proof of the product's value. What
the product is worth is proven by what it does for the people who stayed -
their verbatims, their outcomes - not by how many were moved past a barrier.
